# The Empire and The Quiet — Lore Précis for Development Reference

> "If magic existed in a real world, that stuff would be regulated."

## Core Concept

A continent of approximately 9.5 million people governed by a sophisticated empire now in its 1072nd year (the current year is approximately 1072 AC). The world's single most important structural feature: magical ability (resonance/rarebirth) is completely random with no hereditary pattern. This prevents magical dynasties and makes the entire political system possible. Approximately 1 in 300 people are rareborn, with 8 thread types each appearing in roughly 1 in 2400 people. Prismborn — full spectrum amplifiers — appear at approximately 1 in a million. Of note - in this world fantastical eye colours appear as well, but these mean nothing besides being pretty.

## The Eight Threads

Voidborn/Nullborn (black hair) — dampening and cancellation. Bloommarked (green) — biological growth and organic chemistry. Tidebound (blue) — water and currents. Firelit (red/copper) — heat and fire. Galeheart (white/silver) — air and wind. Stonewrought (brown/grey) — structural and geological. Starlit (gold) — light manipulation. Rosebound (pink) — emotional cohesion and biological maintenance. Hair color is the visible marker of thread type from birth, making concealment difficult. (The correct term is "Bloommarked," never "Bloommark.")

Modern resonance has exactly these eight recognized threads. Pre-war and archaic records sometimes describe impossible or extinct effects — death reversal ("suspended capital sentences pending resonance recovery"), command over monsters, and stranger things — but these are rare, disputed, politically dangerous to discuss, and not available as normal present-day powers. Scholars file them under "lost threads"; the ruins and old shrines are full of hints, and nobody credible claims to understand them.

## History

The Century War ended at Year Zero when two surviving city-states signed a unification compact. The war, generally speaking, was a war of commonborn vs rareborn, with the commonborn eventually winning a war of attrition. In a post-war purge, the empire systematically killed nearly all surviving rareborn including collaborators and surrendered practitioners. From then on, death for rareborn was official policy. The Silent Centuries followed, with the world existing as a more or less normal medieval world slowly repopulating, with rebel factions and rareborn sympathizers and concealers slowly growing in power and posing a threat to the Empire, until the Accord of Threads was signed in year 700.

The Accord — officially a long bureaucratic title, popularly called the Accord of Threads — never names rareborn, resonance, or magic directly. It refers only to "the condition" and "observable through outward and measurable signs." It rescinds death warrants while preserving death penalty for non-compliance. Article VIII states ambiguity resolves in favor of public order — the document's immune system.

Following this becoming law, resonance users have become a sometimes resented, often cloistered, part of society. They must be registered shortly after birth, be educated from ages 12-18 at special academies and very often graduate to work directly for the Empire. Especially over the last 150 years, resonant abilities are leading to a growth in technologies such as resonance-driven rail ("aetherrail"), hardier crops, and more controllable weather.

Nobody remembers what went on before The Century War - certainly society had existed for thousands of years, and there are shrines to old religions, signs of lost threads, and ruins in many places, but nobody alive knows what these are - the war burned down the history that came before it.

Though binding in law from 700 AC, the Accord's enforcement reached the outer provinces unevenly. For generations after ratification, frontier practice lagged central law: registration offices were distant, provincial administration was thin, and rural rareborn families sometimes still hid as their grandparents had. Later instruments — the Integration Accords (which extended working enforcement and registry infrastructure to the outer provinces), the Reform (a recodification of the Accord code; older provisions survive only as "pre-Reform commentary" in registry archives), and successive registry expansions — brought frontier practice progressively closer to the written law. The Empire's thousandth year (1000 AC) was marked by the Millennial Accord, part celebration and part omnibus reaffirmation of imperial law; provincial elders still date their lives by it. When someone in the fens says a person was "born before the Integration Accords," they mean this long, uneven rollout — not that the original Accord did not exist.

Archives occasionally surface "Classification Registers" dated to the decades before 700 AC — before the Accord created lawful registration. What pre-Accord "classification" actually meant is deliberately ambiguous and quietly sinister: kill lists, containment ledgers, provincial intelligence records, proto-registry documents, or some mixture, many later folded without comment into Accord archives. Serious scholars handle such volumes carefully, and district offices prefer not to discuss them.

## Political Structure

The Council of Thirty-Three — the Three Elevens: eleven hereditary noble seats, eleven elected seats (one per region, five year terms), eleven Wisdom Council seats (selected by existing members after 33 years Imperial service, serve until death). The Concordant is the closest they have to a president, though really they are more like a speaker of the house- they vote only to break ties. The Wisdom Council sides with nobles approximately 90% of the time. The elected eleven represent 95% of the population. This representation gap is the system's central unresolved tension.

Eight kingdoms with their own rulers send representatives to the noble eleven. Twin capitals: Lumina (magical/cultural capital, limestone bluff above the Lume river) and Halcyra (administrative capital, western shore of the Cyrmere inland sea).

## The Quiet

700 AC to the present day (approximately 1072 AC) — some 370 years of managed peace. Population tripled from approximately 3 million to 9.5 million. Literacy reached 70% continentally through the Instruction to Twelve Act — that is the formal title; ordinary people call it the Education Act of 871. It provides publicly funded grade schooling up to age 12, open to any family that wishes to enroll its children, rareborn and commonborn alike. The aetherrail — powered by Stonewrought, Galeheart, and Voidborn practitioners — opened its first operational Imperial line, Halcyra to Lumina (the twin capital cities - the 2 surviving cities mentioned above), in approximately 1020 AC: the technology is only about 52 years old, the newest and most visible fruit of the Quiet. Now 11 lines, 47 stations — all of it in the richer northeast. No line or stop serves Calwick or Drenwick; the nearest railhead is three towns east of Calwick (two towns beyond Drenwick), reached by road and coach.

## Economics

Currency: in everyday use across the provinces, the coin is simply "gold" — wages, tolls, shop prices, rewards, and ledgers are all quoted in gold pieces (the "g" of every counter and pay ticket). This is the currency ordinary people use and the game UI shows. Formal Imperial accounting retains older bureaucratic denominations (marks, crowns, sovereigns) in central ledgers and instruments of credit, but a fen clerk can go years without seeing one written down. The Kethrin (a noble family) invented standardized letters of credit and effectively operates as the continental central bank. Do not derive salary or price scales that contradict in-game amounts — as reference points, a canal toll is 1 gold, a potion runs about 30 gold, and a hired district investigation pays 50-200 gold.

The bracelet is a Rosebound/Bloommarked recently invented contraceptive device lasting 40 years; bought legitimately it is costly — several months of a clerk's pay. Generally popular only in high society circles, many nobles fear that it could lead to a decline in morality.

## The Academy System

Formal Academy attendance is compulsory for rareborn youth from roughly ages 12-18 - first in the Junior Academy in The Bowl - a meteor crater at the Brinne/Lume confluence, then at the much older Senior Academy located between Lumina and Halcyra. (Article IV of the Accord established "an Academy," singular, and for centuries the Senior campus was the whole system; the Junior Academy was created later, when the 12-18 rareborn population outgrew a single campus.)

Before age twelve, rareborn children attend ordinary public grade school alongside commonborn children — the Instruction to Twelve Act school system — or no school at all, at their family's discretion. Some families, especially wealthy or status-conscious ones, enroll children far earlier in private schools, including rareborn Academy-prep schools in the major cities. Alecton is the best known of these prep schools: private, expensive, early-entry (children as young as two), with small cohorts of a dozen or so per year. Prep-school testing measures control, strength, temperament, discipline, and administrative risk — never basic thread type, which the hair announces at birth and the registry records shortly after.

The Academy system is simultaneously a genuine educational institution and a sophisticated behavioral observation system. Lantern Night — the graduation celebration — uses intoxicating Elixirs that lower inhibitions while faculty observe. Students who figure this out are not wrong and are not free of it. The surveillance and the genuine gift are the same thing.

## The Rebel Factions

The Reclaimers — populist, commonfolk underrepresentation - rural anger, anti-elitist and sometimes communist. The Unshackled Flame — rareborn supremacists, radical faction believes the Century War was a failed revolution and the wrong side won. These two factions - if they could band together, could threaten the empire, however because they are at loggerheads with each other, they are never able to.

## The World

The Empire governs "The Continent" - the name for the large landmass that (along with its environs) comprises the known world.

Note that The Continent is in the southern hemisphere of its world, therefore it gets warmer as one goes north, and colder as one goes south.

A permanent resonance storm running near the equator blocks travel to the north hemisphere of the world. This is not explained.

Two inland waters matter to the southwest: the Valmere, the much larger inland sea well to the east of Calwick and the namesake of the province, and the Thornmere, the much smaller sea/fen/shallow system on whose margins Calwick and Drenwick actually sit. Locals live on the Thornmere; the Valmere is "the big sea, way out east" that the canal traffic ultimately serves.

The most popular sport among normal folk is river wrestling, where shirtless men wrestle to see whose shoulders touch the water first - it is viewed as rather barbaric by the upper classes. Rules vary considerably by region, venue, level, and appetite for violence: some circuits play simple first-shoulders-under, while others (the Drenwick circuit among them) run codified hold-and-break systems fought in moving, at-least-knee-deep current, where being taken under for long seconds is part of the show.

Monsters exist — fen creatures, remnant things in old ruins, worse things below. Ordinary people call some of their lingering effects "curses," and the word is fair as folk usage; scholars pointedly do NOT classify these phenomena as resonance, and there is no ninth magic system. They are simply hazards nobody has been funded to explain.

## Tone

The Empire is neither good nor bad — genuinely beneficial in many ways, genuinely aging and corrupt in others. No chosen ones. No ancient evil. The world in 1072 AC is five steps from the precipice, not one. The primary everyday concerns are population growth, education, healthcare, political representation — normal problems of a civilization doing well enough to have arrived at the next tier of difficulties. The trains are still marvel for those who get to ride them. The people drink mushroom wine on dayoff. Ten million people living their lives.

## The Game

Browser-based old school JRPG. Player character is a junior IJC Investigator posted to Calwick, a wetlands town of ~800 on the margin of the Thornmere, a cool, small, shallow sea-and-fen system southwest of the capitals' region. (The province is named for the Valmere, the far larger inland sea well to the east — see The World; the Valmere is not local to Calwick.) Calwick's economy: reed harvesting, fishing, agricultural drainage management, ruins documentation when structures emerge from the mud in dry years. Second town: Drenwick, population ~2000, larger and more impersonal. A canal town that will be severely economically impacted if the drought goes on for too long.

As the game starts, the water situation has three distinct layers: the North Basin reservoir system has been quietly declining for years (old waterlines stranded well above the water, mooring posts on dry ground); the current dry spell has lasted three rainless months; and only now has the local office officially classified it as a drought. The cause is unknown. Nobody's dying, but people are starting to get nervous.
