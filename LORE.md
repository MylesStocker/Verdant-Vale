# The Empire and The Quiet — Lore Précis for Development Reference

> "If magic existed in a real world, that stuff would be regulated."

## Core Concept

A continent of approximately 9.5 million people governed by a sophisticated empire now in its 1040th year. The world's single most important structural feature: magical ability (resonance/rarebirth) is completely random with no hereditary pattern. This prevents magical dynasties and makes the entire political system possible. Approximately 1 in 300 people are rareborn, with 8 thread types each appearing in roughly 1 in 2400 people. Prismborn — full spectrum amplifiers — appear at approximately 1 in a million. Of note - in this world fantastical eye colours appear as well, but these mean nothing besides being pretty.

## The Eight Threads

Voidborn/Nullborn (black hair) — dampening and cancellation. Bloommarked (green) — biological growth and organic chemistry. Tidebound (blue) — water and currents. Firelit (red/copper) — heat and fire. Galeheart (white/silver) — air and wind. Stonewrought (brown/grey) — structural and geological. Starlit (gold) — light manipulation. Rosebound (pink) — emotional cohesion and biological maintenance. Hair color is the visible marker of thread type from birth, making concealment difficult.

## History

The Century War ended at Year Zero when two surviving city-states signed a unification compact. The war, generally speaking, was a war of commonborn vs rareborn, with the commonborn eventually winning a war of attrition. In a post-war purge, the empire systematically killed nearly all surviving rareborn including collaborators and surrendered practitioners. From then on, death for rareborn was official policy. The Silent Centuries followed, with the world existing as a more or less normal medieval world slowly repopulating, with rebel factions and rareborn sympathizers and concealers slowly growing in power and posing a threat to the Empire, until the Accord of Threads was signed in year 700.

The Accord — officially a long bureaucratic title, popularly called the Accord of Threads — never names rareborn, resonance, or magic directly. It refers only to "the condition" and "observable through outward and measurable signs." It rescinds death warrants while preserving death penalty for non-compliance. Article VIII states ambiguity resolves in favor of public order — the document's immune system.

Following this becoming law, resonance users have become a sometimes resented, often cloistered, part of society. They must be registered shortly after birth, be educated from ages 12-18 at special academies and very often graduate to work directly for the Empire. Especially over the last 150 years, resonant abilities are leading to a growth in technologies such as resonance-driven rail ("aetherrail"), hardier crops, and more controllable weather.

Nobody remembers what went on before The Century War - certainly society had existed for thousands of years, and there are shrines to old religions, signs of lost threads, and ruins in many places, but nobody alive knows what these are - the war burned down the history that came before it.

## Political Structure

The Council of Thirty-Three — the Three Elevens: eleven hereditary noble seats, eleven elected seats (one per region, five year terms), eleven Wisdom Council seats (selected by existing members after 33 years Imperial service, serve until death). The Concordant is the closest they have to a president, though really they are more like a speaker of the house- they vote only to break ties. The Wisdom Council sides with nobles approximately 90% of the time. The elected eleven represent 95% of the population. This representation gap is the system's central unresolved tension.

Eight kingdoms with their own rulers send representatives to the noble eleven. Twin capitals: Lumina (magical/cultural capital, limestone bluff above the Lume river) and Halcyra (administrative capital, western shore of the Cyrmere inland sea).

## The Quiet

700 AC to 1040 AC — 340 years of managed peace. Population tripled from approximately 3 million to 9.5 million. Literacy reached 70% continentally by 1040 AC through the Instruction to Twelve Act (free education to age 12). The aetherrail — powered by Stonewrought, Galeheart, and Voidborn practitioners — opened its first line Lumina to Halcyra (the twin capital cities - the 2 surviving cities mentioned above) in 940 AC. Now 11 lines, 47 stations.

## Economics

Currency: marks (10 pennies), crowns (10 marks), sovereigns (100 marks). The Kethrin (a noble family) invented standardized letters of credit and effectively operates as the continental central bank. Peasant agricultural laborer earns 40-60 marks per year cash. Junior Imperial clerk 180-220 marks. Senior administrator 480-520 marks.

The bracelet is a Rosebound/Bloommarked recently invented contraceptive device lasting 40 years, costs 180-220 marks legitimate. Generally popular only in high society circles, many nobles fear that it could lead to a decline in morality.

## The Academy System

Rareborn youth must enrol in Imperial education from ages 12-18 - first in the Junior Academy in The Bowl - a meteor crater at the Brinne/Lume confluence, then at the much older Senior Academy located between Lumina and Halcyra. The Academy system is simultaneously a genuine educational institution and a sophisticated behavioral observation system. Lantern Night — the graduation celebration — uses intoxicating Elixirs that lower inhibitions while faculty observe. Students who figure this out are not wrong and are not free of it. The surveillance and the genuine gift are the same thing.

## The Rebel Factions

The Reclaimers — populist, commonfolk underrepresentation - rural anger, anti-elitist and sometimes communist. The Unshackled Flame — rareborn supremacists, radical faction believes the Century War was a failed revolution and the wrong side won. These two factions - if they could band together, could threaten the empire, however because they are at loggerheads with each other, they are never able to.

## The World

The Empire governs "The Continent" - the name for the large landmass that (along with its environs) comprises the known world.

Note that The Continent is in the southern hemisphere of its world, therefore it gets warmer as one goes north, and colder as one goes south.

A permanent resonance storm running near the equator blocks travel to the north hemisphere of the world. This is not explained.

The most popular sport among normal folk is river wrestling, where shirtless men wrestle to see whose shoulders touch the water first - it is viewed as rather barbaric by the upper classes.

## Tone

The Empire is neither good nor bad — genuinely beneficial in many ways, genuinely aging and corrupt in others. No chosen ones. No ancient evil. The world in 1040 AC is five steps from the precipice, not one. The primary everyday concerns are population growth, education, healthcare, political representation — normal problems of a civilization doing well enough to have arrived at the next tier of difficulties. The trains are still marvel for those who get to ride them. The people drink mushroom wine on dayoff. Ten million people living their lives.

## The Game

Browser-based old school JRPG. Player character is a junior IJC Investigator posted to Calwick, a wetlands town of ~800 on the southwest margin of the Valmere, a cool shallow inland sea southwest of the capitals' region. Calwick's economy: reed harvesting, fishing, agricultural drainage management, ruins documentation when structures emerge from the mud in dry years. Second town: Drenwick, population ~2000, larger and more impersonal. A canal town that will be severely economically impacted if the drought goes on for too long.

As the game starts, the area is experiencing a once-in-a-forever drought, for unknown reason. Nobody's dying, but people are starting to get nervous.
